# Characters and crews

Version 2 adds six workplace-friendly characters in the [Studio Collection](PROFESSIONAL-COLLECTION.md), bringing the full roster to 18 identities and nine crew recipes. The 12 below are the original playful collection.

## The shared character language

Every folk has one recognizable silhouette, one identifying accessory, and a readable face at roughly 140 px tall. The floating light belongs to the **agent state**, not the species. Names and project labels stay visible on questions. The same pet can represent any agent or project; the roles below are personality suggestions, not technical abilities or automatic routing.

The first trio crosses every style. Later characters can cross styles after their debut rather than requiring every possible asset combination at launch.

## Character direction cards

**Pip — the welcoming anchor.** Approaches with a small two-step hop, then holds still while you read. Thinking folds one ear; waiting raises the right paw and sign; done is one brief hop. Voice: “The tests are ready to run.” Keep the current ivory coat, dark extremities and familiar ear shape. Do not make the hero depend on a complicated outfit.

**Byte — the thoughtful investigator.** Leans toward a task, pauses, then looks back at you. Thinking tilts the head behind the glasses; waiting shows the same right-paw sign; done is a small satisfied nod. Voice: “One check needs your answer.” Keep the glasses clear of the eyes and the hooked tail readable in silhouette. Nerdy through curiosity, not walls of code or hacker stereotypes.

**Patch — the steady builder.** Has a slightly wider stance and energetic walk. Working alternates simple paw movements; waiting plants both feet; done gives one tail flick. Voice: “The fix is ready to review.” No permanent tools in the approval hand; tool belts from the concept board are optional large-scale illustration details.

**Loom — the patient organizer.** Gentle body squash, slow blinks, small head turns. Reading settles into a seated pose; waiting lifts a paw cleanly outside the wool; done straightens the scarf knot. Voice: “Your next step is here.” Wool is a few broad shapes, not hundreds of curls.

**Moss — the grounded caretaker.** Moves deliberately without making the UI respond slowly. Working is a steady head bob; failed lowers the head while keeping the face visible; done is a firm nod. Voice: “This run needs a look.” The shell can be rigid on the torso. The full face must remain visible during important states.

**Relay — the direct communicator.** Curious head pivot and short wave. Thinking uses eye direction, not scrolling screen text; waiting holds the sign; done shows a smile. Voice: “A reply is waiting in sidefolk.” Screen expressions decorate the state; they never replace a real permission card.

**Stack — the careful archivist.** Tucks the book away before asking you anything. Reading sits squarely; waiting opens one palm; done closes the book. Voice: “The notes are ready.” Limit tail stripes to three broad bands and keep the overshirt as a single shape at small sizes.

**Nib — the playful designer.** Four rounded tentacle shapes, one dedicated to props, with restrained squash and stretch. Thinking tips the cap; waiting points upward; done makes one small bounce. Voice: “Two directions are ready to compare.” Begin as 2D: forcing a squid into the current two-arm/two-leg rig should not be the first production task.

**Pico — the friendly tinkerer.** A single rectangle makes it distinguishable from animals. Working rocks a few degrees; failed eyes soften; waiting extends a mitten arm. Voice: “The tool stopped here.” Use static screen eyes; no continuous CRT flicker or noisy scanline shader.

**Sora — the pocket navigator.** Adult character represented with chibi proportions; practical jacket and trousers. Working checks a plain folder; waiting raises the right hand; done offers a quick nod. Voice: “Your project is ready for the next step.” Production target is about 2.5 heads tall, with short hair and no long trailing cloth.

**Yuna — the quiet archivist.** Minimal gesture, clear gaze, compact cape. Reading brings a small book close; waiting opens the right palm; done closes the eyes briefly. Voice: “The research is ready.” Keep the crescent clip decorative, separate from the status signal. Avoid detailed jewelry and large magical effects.

**Kite — the curious explorer.** Rounded dragon, no sharp threatening shapes. Working leans forward; waiting lifts a forepaw; done gives one compact wing tuck. Voice: “The prototype is ready to try.” Wings stay folded in the first build. Flying, wing physics and extra joints are separate scope.

All sample lines are tone examples. Actual content comes from real events or an agent message; the pet never claims work succeeded just because it played an animation.

## Six possible crews

Each recipe has one main pet plus three possible project companions. These are suggested configurations, not instructions to keep all four visible. Keep current den behavior; send idle companions home.

| Crew | Main | Project companions | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter Studio** · recommended | Pip | Byte: code, Patch: web, Loom: docs | Warm, coherent Soft 3D |
| **Midnight Lab** | Byte | Pico: tools, Relay: integrations, Moss: infrastructure | Nerdy, low distraction |
| **Ink Workshop** | Pip | Stack: knowledge, Nib: design, Pico: experiments | Tactile, flat 2D |
| **Pocket Guild** | Sora | Yuna: research, Kite: prototypes, Pip: inbox | Gentle original anime |
| **Quiet Garden** | Moss | Loom: planning, Pip: main project, Byte: research | Calm, low-motion |
| **Mixed Desk** | Pip in 3D | Byte in Ink, Patch in Anime, Relay in 3D | Personal choice, shared UI |

**Available-base starter:** Pip (bunny / snow / none), Byte (cat / midnight / glasses), Patch (fox / ember / none). These use existing look keys but are only approximations of the illustrated palettes. Exact charcoal/mint Byte and terracotta/mint Patch need new palette work. Loom and the expanded crews are concepts.

## Style families

| Family | Shape / finish | Animation approach | Guardrails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Soft 3D** | Round volumes, matte surfaces, controlled toon shading | Existing skeletal clips, tiny gaze shifts | Simplify generated fur; no simulated fibers or cloth |
| **Ink & Geek** | Strong charcoal line, flat warm fills, restrained paper flavor in marketing art | Authored sprite clips or cutout rig | Desktop sprites have transparent backgrounds; no baked paper rectangle |
| **Pocket Anime** | Clean cel shading, expressive eyes, two shadow tones | 2D first; 3D toon adaptation later | Original designs, compact proportions, no screen-filling effects |

Possible later skins: crisp pixel art, monochrome terminal, low-poly. These are backlog ideas, not illustrated or implemented in this package. A minimal high-contrast silhouette mode would be useful across all families.
